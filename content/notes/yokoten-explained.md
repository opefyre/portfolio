Yokoten means spreading learning or a useful practice across areas. In Lean work, it includes understanding the practice and adapting it to the receiving operation.

Copying a board or device captures its visible form. The receiving team also needs the operating rules and support that made the original useful.

[José R. Ferro's LEI article](https://www.lean.org/the-lean-post/articles/yokoten-capturing-and-sharing-best-practices/) illustrates this through andon. He describes companies copying the signal while lacking clear instructions, response responsibilities or the trust needed to use it. He presents it as a practitioner account.

## Transfer the response behind the signal

The [jidoka and andon Note](/notes/jidoka-andon-explained/) uses a fictional washer-presence check. The signal needs a defined hold, someone to respond and evidence for restart.

Those conditions give a receiving team concrete details to inspect. Installing the same light at another station would not establish that its hold method, support route and restart authority fit that station.

::figure aici-yokoten-transfer | Transfer the problem, mechanism and conditions along with the visible practice. The receiving team needs its own checked operating response.

| Transfer record | What the receiving team needs to understand |
| --- | --- |
| Problem addressed | The condition the original practice was intended to change |
| Mechanism | How the practice changed the work |
| Evidence | Results and limitations of the original use |
| Required conditions | Skills, equipment, authority and support on which it depends |
| Local adaptation | Differences that require a changed method |
| Verification | How the receiving team will check its result |

## Compare the conditions before promising the result

Observe the original work and talk with the people who operate it. Ask about exceptions, replenishment, support and the parts of the method that a photograph cannot show.

Then inspect the receiving process. Preserve requirements that still apply and test changes where local conditions differ. A transferred result should remain a prediction until the receiving operation supplies its own evidence.

The [PDCA guide](/notes/pdca-cycle-example/) explains that trial and review. Vrolen's [andon field guide](https://vrolen.com/blog/andon-system-manufacturing/) gives a separate view of response records and escalation, which is relevant when the transferred practice involves a help signal.

## Keep project lessons connected but distinct

A [lessons-learned record](/notes/lessons-learned-example/) can identify the practice another team should examine. Yokoten concerns the further work of understanding, adapting and using it across operations.

Preserve the local changes in the receiving team's standard. If they discover a better way to handle a condition, return that learning to the originating team too.

For the washer example, take the hold rule, responder and restart check into the receiving-station review. Check those three operating details before purchasing a matching light.
