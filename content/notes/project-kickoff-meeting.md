A project kickoff meeting brings the people delivering and receiving the work together to agree how they will begin. The team confirms the assignment, checks responsibilities and dependencies, and records decisions or unresolved points that affect delivery.

The meeting needs an authorised project to start from. It does not give the team new spending or resource authority just because everyone attended. The [project charter](/notes/project-charter-example/) explains that approval.

For the kickoff below, use the fictional barcode pilot in the [work breakdown structure Note](/notes/work-breakdown-structure-example/): one warehouse zone, 120 bins, receiving and picking transactions, and users on two shifts.

## Put the agreed result in front of the team

The pilot needs approved labels, configured scanners, a tested interface to the existing enterprise resource planning system, trained users and named support. ERP replacement and other warehouses sit outside its scope.

That is enough to start a concrete discussion. “Install scanners” would leave the integration and handover hidden.

[PMI's practitioner paper on project starts](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/train-wrecks-ensure-project-right-track-7531) describes kickoff work around shared scope, outputs, roles and the delivery approach. The agenda here applies that purpose to the existing pilot; it is not a prescribed PMI meeting template.

## Project kickoff agenda and expected outputs

Give participants the scope and open decisions beforehand. Use the meeting to resolve them, rather than reading a project document aloud.

| Agenda item | Question for this pilot | Record to leave behind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose and boundary | Which zone, transactions and shifts does the pilot include? | Confirmed scope and exclusions |
| Acceptance | What proves a received or picked quantity reaches the correct item and bin once? | Agreed checks and the person accepting the result |
| Delivery sequence | Which labels, data and configuration must exist before interface testing? | Dependencies and owners to confirm dates |
| Responsibilities | Who supplies warehouse access, test equipment and integration work? | Confirmed commitments or an open allocation decision |
| Operating support | Who responds when a scan or posting fails on either shift? | Support owner and evidence still needed |
| Decisions and follow-up | Which points block the next activity? | Decision owner, due date and next check |

For the interface package, the existing acceptance conditions include retrying a failed transfer without creating a duplicate. Ask the integration and warehouse leads to confirm how they will test that case.

If either lead cannot commit the required access or time, record the unresolved dependency. A person's name in a responsibility table does not reserve their capacity.

## Invite people because of the decisions they own

The project manager needs the people who define, build, approve and operate the affected work. For this pilot, that means the warehouse representative, integration lead, relevant system owner and people responsible for training and support. Involve the sponsor where authority or competing commitments need a decision.

The team should hear from both shifts covered by the pilot. A day-only discussion can miss access or support conditions that change overnight.

Use the [RACI comparison](/notes/raci-matrix-guide/) to separate doing the work from accepting it. Keep the wider stakeholder list in the [stakeholder map](/notes/stakeholder-mapping-example/); everyone on that list does not need to attend every planning discussion.

## Choose the length from what remains unresolved

There is no universal kickoff duration. A familiar team with an agreed scope may need a short alignment meeting. Unclear transaction boundaries or conflicting resource commitments need preparation and possibly separate working sessions.

End a discussion with a decision, or with the person and evidence needed to reach one. Do not fill a fixed meeting slot by adding presentations that change no part of the work.

An unresolved overnight support arrangement should leave the room as an owned action. It should not become a sentence saying “support discussed.”

## Send a short record that people can act on

After the meeting, circulate the confirmed scope, decisions, owners and dates. Link the current WBS, acceptance record and schedule rather than copying them into several competing documents.

If the kickoff exposes a request outside the agreed zone, follow [change control](/notes/scope-creep-change-control/) before adding it. Update the plan once the responsible people have confirmed the resulting commitments.

Before interface testing starts, check that the agreed labels, test data, equipment and responsible people are available. Those are the commitments this kickoff should make easier to verify.
