**New product introduction, or NPI, is the coordinated work of taking a new product from its agreed definition into production and market supply.** It connects design, sourcing, planning, manufacturing, quality and the teams responsible for the launch.

Commercial approval is one decision in that sequence. It does not show that the right packaging exists, that a supplier is qualified or that the production process can make the required quantity.

A launch plan needs to connect those conditions before the factory commits its scarce line time.

## NPI and product development overlap, with different questions

Product development establishes and verifies the product design. NPI brings that design into the work needed to source, make, release and supply it. The boundaries vary by organisation and product, so agree responsibilities rather than relying on the department names.

[Oracle's product-development documentation](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/supply-chain-and-manufacturing/26c/oaimi/an-introduction-to-innovation-management.html) shows one formal connection from approved concepts through designs and bills of material into manufacturing. [NASA's product-transition guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/5-5-product-transition/) also checks the receiving operation, people and supporting resources. For a factory, that includes the material, production method and people who will run it.

## Build the readiness map around the first production lot

In this fictional example, a factory plans a first saleable lot of 5,000 units in week 6. The new product uses a revised package and an existing filling line. The commercial team has approved the launch proposal.

The introduction team checks what must be true for that specific lot.

| Readiness area | Evidence needed for this launch | Current position at the week-3 review | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product definition | Approved specification and bill of materials | Approved | Product lead |
| Packaging | Approved artwork, fit and supplier confirmation | Artwork correction open | Packaging lead |
| Supplier and materials | Qualified source and released stock for trial and first lot | Production delivery depends on final artwork | Procurement and planning leads |
| Production process | Representative trial and agreed operating method | Trial booked, not run | Manufacturing lead |
| Quality release | Required checks, results and release authority | Plan agreed; results pending | Quality lead |
| Demand and capacity | Agreed initial quantity and feasible line slot | 5,000 units proposed; slot provisional | Planning lead |
| Distribution | Released finished stock, labels and shipment arrangements | To be checked after lot release | Logistics lead |

The table is a fictional review snapshot, not a universal launch checklist. Product regulation, safety and customer requirements can add mandatory evidence that cannot be traded away for an earlier date.

## Put the dependencies into the schedule

Artwork approval is needed before the supplier prints the production packaging. Released packaging is needed before a representative packing trial. Trial findings must be resolved before quality and manufacturing approve saleable production.

The first lot also needs capacity and released material. If the artwork moves by a week, the production slot does not become feasible merely because it remains coloured green in the plan.

Early trials with development material may still answer useful questions. Record which questions they answer and which remain open. A trial on a substitute package does not automatically qualify the final printed package or its supplier.

## Separate a trial decision from a production-release decision

At week 4, the team may approve a controlled engineering trial with an agreed quantity and a plan for what happens to the trial output. That is different from approving the first saleable lot.

For the saleable lot, the decision owner reviews the actual evidence, open issues and consequences. If a required condition is missing, the options may include correcting it, changing the launch scope or moving the date. An unqualified supplier or unresolved product requirement should not disappear inside a general “accepted risk” label.

The [stage-gate example](/notes/stage-gate-review-example/) explains decision authority. [3P production preparation](/notes/3p-production-preparation-process/) examines how to design and test the production method. NPI connects those decisions to material, supplier and market readiness.

## Check capacity against the new product's evidence

An existing product runs at 1,000 good units a day. That does not establish the new package can do the same. Use representative trial evidence, including setup, quality losses, staffing and the effect on other committed work.

The first 5,000-unit lot is a demand and launch decision as well as a production calculation. Challenge an optimistic volume before buying material that will become obsolete if the launch changes. The [demand-forecasting methods note](/notes/demand-forecasting-methods/) discusses how the question and available evidence affect the forecast approach.

In my [earlier note on launch flow](/notes/busy-machines-slower-orders/), I described how readiness checkpoints and cross-functional governance helped reduce launch lead time in my own work. Starting fewer half-ready launches was part of that change. The readiness review above follows the same principle: check whether the work can flow before releasing it.

## Keep NPI open through the initial operating check

Shipping the first lot does not establish stable production. Agree the period and evidence for the early-life review: actual output, quality, supply performance, remaining support and any customer issues relevant to this product.

Transfer the accepted method, records, unresolved actions and named owners through the [handover process](/notes/project-handover-checklist/). The receiving operation should know what to do when the launch team is no longer in the room.

For this product, week 6 remains a proposal until the required conditions support it. A readiness map lets the team see which work can proceed now and which commitment still needs evidence.
