**Lean daily management is the routine for comparing actual work with the plan, responding to problems and checking whether the response worked.** A board helps people see the gap. The management routine determines what happens next.

The packing team has written “material shortage” beside a missed target for three mornings. The board is up to date, but the shortage keeps happening.

At today's review, the supervisor asks which material, when it was needed and what prevented it reaching the line. That gives the team something it can act on.

## What belongs on a daily management board?

In this fictional factory, the team reviews safety concerns, quality issues, output against the agreed plan and the actions still open. The board carries only information used in that conversation. Safety or quality concerns that need an immediate response do not wait for the meeting.

Here is the output part of the board. The plan already allows for the scheduled break outside these windows.

| Window | Planned good packs | Actual good packs | Gap | Recorded observation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 08:00 to 09:00 | 100 | 100 | 0 | Normal run |
| 09:00 to 10:00 | 100 | 75 | −25 | Carton C17 unavailable at line for 15 minutes |
| 10:00 to 11:00 | 100 | 95 | −5 | Restart adjustment recorded |
| Total | 300 | 270 | **−30** | Two separate observations to investigate |

The missing 30 packs are measured. “Carton unavailable” describes an observed condition. It does not yet establish why the carton was missing or explain every lost pack.

[The Lean Enterprise Institute describes](https://www.lean.org/explore-lean/line-management/) daily management as work that requires a response from managers, including help with abnormalities and escalation. Keeping a red cell visible without support leaves that work unfinished.

## Use the short review to agree a response

The team confirms that the next production order needs the same carton. Stores has enough stock, so the stores lead arranges a controlled delivery before that order starts. That protects the next order while the team investigates why the delivery was missed.

The team also finds that the pick list was issued after the planned material delivery time. A screen shows the order as ready even when the carton check is incomplete. The supervisor cannot change that shared planning rule at the line.

The action record therefore separates today's protection from the recurring problem.

| Action | Owner | Due or escalation point | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deliver C17 for the next released order | Stores lead | Before 11:20 start | Required quantity physically available |
| Review why the pick list missed the delivery window | Planning lead | Next morning's review | Order release and pick-list timestamps |
| Resolve responsibility for the readiness check | Operations manager | Escalate at 14:00 if still unassigned | Agreed owner and tested release rule |

“Improve communication” would leave all three tasks unclear. The record above tells the next shift what should have happened and where to look.

## Tiered meetings should pass a decision, not repeat the board

In a tiered routine, the frontline review handles what the team can resolve. The next management level takes problems needing wider authority, resources or coordination. More tiers are useful only when they make those decisions easier.

For C17, the escalation carries the affected order, the timing evidence, the immediate cover and the unresolved ownership decision. The operations manager does not need a second reading of every green number on the board.

This also gives the manager a specific obligation: confirm who will resolve the shared readiness rule and when the team will receive an answer.

## Check the next order before closing the action

Tomorrow's review checks the physical delivery and the timestamps. If C17 arrived on time but the next order still lacked another material, the team has contained one incident without fixing the release process.

A repeated problem may need a [root cause investigation](/notes/root-cause-analysis-explained/) and a small tested change. Daily management provides the evidence and follow-up; a ten-minute meeting does not replace that investigation.

Once a change is accepted, update the [standard work](/notes/standard-work-vs-sop/) and check it under ordinary conditions. Closing an action because an email was sent would confuse activity with a result.

## Keep the routine useful to the people doing the work

Leader standard work can specify the manager's own checks: review open escalations, visit the affected work and return with the promised decision. It should make support dependable, rather than create another inspection of whether the board looks tidy.
